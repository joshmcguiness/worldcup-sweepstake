## Stage 1 — how far behind the market is our Elo?

The same Elo the live site uses, rebuilt match-by-match in date order (25%
regression to the mean between seasons), scored against the closing market.

| Set | n | Elo log-loss | Market log-loss | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 2021–2026 | 1,160 | 0.6352 | 0.6084 | **+0.027** |
| Settled 2023–2026 (Elo warmed up) | 773 | 0.6560 | 0.6460 | **+0.010** |

**Read:** the market is meaningfully ahead of our Elo (lower log-loss = better).
Once the ratings have two seasons to settle, the gap narrows to ~0.010 log-loss —
small but real, and exactly the gap Mission B asks whether SuperCoach lineup
value can close. The market's calibration is good through the 0.3–0.8 range and
only wobbles in the sparse tails (n<30 per bin). This is the honest baseline:
**our job is not to beat some naive line, it's to claw back that 0.01–0.027.**

## Interpretation — a "no", but a *promising* no

The strict pre-registered rule returns **no**, and we honour it: **scDiff is not
being wired into the live engine.** But the failure is the interesting kind, and
three things are worth recording before anyone concludes SuperCoach is useless:

1. **The direction is right and consistent.** The scDiff coefficient is positive
   in every held-out season (more lineup value → more likely to win). Nothing
   flipped sign; the model just isn't *confident* enough to clear the bar.
2. **It's getting better, not worse.** M2 beat M1 in 2025 and 2026 (and the 2026
   CI only barely includes zero, lo = −0.001). It *hurt* in 2024. Two of three
   seasons help, trending up — consistent with "real but weak signal swamped by
   noise in a 200-match season," which is exactly what a bigger sample would
   resolve. It is **not** consistent enough to bet on yet.
3. **The gains cluster in Origin rounds — which is the trap, not the prize.**
   M2 beat M1 on the Origin subset in 2025 (0.606 vs 0.621) and 2026 (0.595 vs
   0.641). Tempting — but the literature (roadmap §3.0) says Origin depletion is
   already priced (R²≈0.05). So this is exactly the result we agreed to distrust,
   and 2024's Origin subset contradicts it (0.796 vs 0.742) anyway.

**Ignore the ROI column.** ROIs of 4.2 / 14.0 / 0.03 with CIs spanning
[−0.12, 12.9] etc. are pure noise — a handful of longshots at exchange prices.
This is the roadmap's point that raw P/L needs thousands of bets while log-loss
needs ~50; trust the log-loss columns, not the profit sim.

### The honest caveat that keeps the door open

Our scDiff is a **proxy**: "sum of the best-available-17 by price," excluding
players the fantasy feed marked injured/suspended/not-playing. It is **not** the
actual named 17 from Team List Tuesday. The real named lineup (NRL match-centre
`/data`, roadmap §3.4) would sharpen the who's-actually-out signal that is the
whole point. So the correct statement is:

> *A price-weighted best-available-17 proxy does not beat Elo on 2023–2026 NRL by
> enough to bet on. Before concluding SuperCoach has no edge, re-run with the
> actual named lineups and 2–3 more seasons.*

That is the recommended next experiment — not wiring scDiff into the engine.

## Final synthesis — three tests, one honest verdict: NO

We now have three independent runs of the SuperCoach hypothesis:

| Test | lineup measure | leak? | scDiff coef | M2 beats M1? | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NRL Stage 2 | best-available-17 by price | none | +3.0 to +5.3 | 2 of 3 seasons | no |
| NRL Stage 2b | the 17 who **played** | leaky (upper bound) | +0.06 to +0.84 | ~0 (flat) | no |
| AFL Stage 2 | the ~22 who **played** | near-none | +3.1 to +3.8 | 5 of 5 seasons | no |

**All three say NO, and reading them together makes the "no" cleaner, not
softer.** The key is NRL Stage 2b: when we value the *actual* NRL 17 (the
strongest case for the hypothesis — hindsight lineups), the effect **collapses
to nothing** — coefficients near zero, loss-gain CIs sitting on zero. That tells
us the small positive lean in the NRL *proxy* (Stage 2) was mostly squad quality
that Elo already knows, not extra lineup information. Value the real lineup and
the extra signal is gone.

AFL is the one code that keeps a consistent (still-insignificant) positive lean
even on the played-lineup measure. So if there is any exploitable SuperCoach
edge, the evidence points to it being **AFL-specific and small** — plausibly
because AFL scoring is more dispersed across a 22 than NRL's tight 17, so who
plays moves the needle more. NRL, on the cleanest test, shows nothing.

**Decision:** do not wire scDiff into either engine. The World Cup discipline
holds — we bet Elo + market with the Mission-A diagnosis, and the value board
surfaces where they disagree. The one experiment that could still flip AFL to a
"yes" is a pooled, multi-season fixed-effects model on AFL played-lineup scDiff
(more statistical power than any single 190-match season); until that clears
zero, SuperCoach stays a studied-and-parked idea, honestly documented here.
